Q2 (b)

Not entirely. Fixed costs that stay the same under every option are irrelevant. Fixed costs become relevant when: (1) the decision itself changes them, e.g., extra supervision or rent for a special order, or a step-up in fixed costs when capacity is expanded; (2) they can be avoided, e.g., on closing down a segment or shutting a plant temporarily; (3) they are incurred specifically for the proposal, e.g., hiring a machine or a licence fee; (4) in make-or-buy decisions, where buying lets the firm avoid some fixed costs; (5) in long-run pricing and capacity decisions, where all costs must be recovered; (6) as opportunity costs of committed facilities that could be let out.

Q3 (b)

Principles of BPR (Hammer): (1) organise around outcomes, not tasks; (2) let those who use the output of a process perform the process; (3) build information processing into the real work that produces the information; (4) treat geographically dispersed resources as if they were centralised; (5) link parallel activities instead of integrating their results; (6) put the decision point where the work is performed, and build control into the process; (7) capture information once, at the source.

Q4 (b)

Steps in implementing TQM: (1) identify customers and customer groups, using a team approach such as multi-voting; (2) identify what customers expect; (3) identify the products or services that meet those expectations; (4) identify the processes that deliver them; (5) set up measures of performance and benchmarks; (6) find the root causes of problems, e.g., with Pareto analysis or fishbone diagrams; (7) improve processes continuously (Kaizen, PDCA); (8) train and empower employees, with top-management commitment throughout.

Q5 (a)

Spare capacity of A = 50,000 − 40,000 = 10,000 units.
(i) For 15,000 units: 10,000 units at VC ₹60 = 6,00,000. The other 5,000 units displace outside sales, so each costs VC 60 + lost contribution 40 = ₹100, giving 5,00,000. Minimum TP = 11,00,000 / 15,000 = ₹73.33 per unit.
(ii) Look at each block. The first 10,000 units cost ₹60 to make inside against ₹95 outside, so make them inside. The next 5,000 have an opportunity cost of ₹100 against ₹95 outside, so buy them outside.
Cost to the company: transfer all 15,000 = ₹11,00,000; buy all 15,000 = ₹14,25,000; best plan (transfer 10,000, buy 5,000) = 6,00,000 + 4,75,000 = ₹10,75,000. This saves ₹25,000 against transferring everything, and ₹3,50,000 against buying everything.
(iii) For the 10,000 units transferred, any TP from ₹60 to ₹95 leaves both divisions no worse off.

Q5 (b)

Price sensitivity is how much the price of a product affects buyers' purchasing behaviour. It is measured through price elasticity of demand: the percentage change in quantity divided by the percentage change in price. Factors: availability of substitutes; whether the product is a necessity or a luxury; share of the buyer's income spent on it; brand loyalty and differentiation; switching costs; whether the buyer is the payer (e.g., expense-account purchases); perceived quality ("price–quality effect"); the time period, since demand is more elastic in the long run; and how easily prices can be compared (internet).

(i) BI software: a set of applications that collect, integrate, analyse and present business data, such as dashboards, reports, OLAP, data mining and ad-hoc queries.
